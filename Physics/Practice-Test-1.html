Question 1Electric Charges and Fields
Point charges +9 μC and −4 μC are fixed at x = 0 and x = 10 cm on the x-axis. At which point on the x-axis is the net electric field zero?
  1. x = 6 cm
  2. x = 18 cm
  3. x = −30 cm
  4. x = 30 cm
Concept to recallFor unlike charges the null point lies outside the pair, on the side of the smaller magnitude; set kq1/r12 = kq2/r22.
  1. Between the charges both fields point the same way (towards −4 μC), so E ≠ 0 there.
  2. Left of +9 μC the larger charge is nearer, so its field always wins. The point must be at x > 10 cm.
  3. 9/x2 = 4/(x − 10)2 ⇒ 3(x − 10) = 2x ⇒ x = 30 cm.

Answer: option (4) — x = 30 cm

Trapx = 6 cm solves 3(10 − x) = 2x, i.e. puts the point between unlike charges; 18 cm uses 1/r instead of 1/r2; −30 cm places the point beside the larger charge.
Question 2Electric Charges and Fields
A point charge of 8.85 μC is placed at one corner of a cube. The electric flux through the cube is (ε0 = 8.85 × 10−12 C2 N−1 m−2)
  1. 1.25 × 105 N m2 C−1
  2. 1.0 × 106 N m2 C−1
  3. 1.67 × 105 N m2 C−1
  4. 4.17 × 104 N m2 C−1
Concept to recallA charge at a corner is shared by 8 identical cubes that together enclose it, so one cube receives Φ = q/(8ε0).
  1. q/ε0 = 8.85 × 10−6/8.85 × 10−12 = 106 N m2 C−1.
  2. Eight cubes meet at a corner: Φ = 106/8 = 1.25 × 105 N m2 C−1.

Answer: option (1) — 1.25 × 105 N m2 C−1

Trap106 is q/ε0 (charge taken as enclosed); 1.67 × 105 is q/6ε0 (the per-face flux of a charge at the centre); 4.17 × 104 is q/24ε0, the flux through one of the three far faces.
Question 3Electric Charges and Fields
Consider the following statements about electrostatic field lines:
  1. (A) Two field lines never cross each other.
  2. (B) Electrostatic field lines form closed loops.
  3. (C) Field lines start on positive charges and end on negative charges (or at infinity).
  4. (D) At the surface of a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium, field lines are normal to the surface.
  5. (E) Where field lines are crowded together the field is weaker.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (C), (D) and (E) only
  2. (A), (D) and (E) only
  3. (A), (C) and (D) only
  4. (A), (B) and (C) only
Concept to recallField lines: tangent = direction of E, density = strength, never cross (E is unique at a point), never closed (E is conservative), normal to an equipotential conductor surface.
  1. (A) true: two tangents at one point would mean two directions of E.
  2. (B) false: a closed loop would give non-zero ∮E·dl, impossible for an electrostatic field.
  3. (C) true and (D) true (a tangential component would drive surface charges).
  4. (E) false: crowded lines mean a stronger field.

Answer: option (3) — (A), (C) and (D) only

TrapClosed loops belong to magnetic field lines and induced electric fields, not electrostatic ones.
Question 4Electric Charges and Fields
Two identical small conducting spheres carry charges +6 μC and −2 μC and attract each other with a force F when kept a distance r apart. They are touched together and put back at the same distance. The force between them now is
  1. F/3, attractive
  2. 4F/3, repulsive
  3. 4F/3, attractive
  4. F/3, repulsive
Concept to recallIdentical conductors in contact share the algebraic sum of their charges equally; F ∝ |q1q2| at a fixed distance.
  1. Total charge = +6 − 2 = +4 μC, so each sphere gets +2 μC.
  2. F′/F = (2 × 2)/(6 × 2) = 1/3.
  3. Both are now positive, so the force is repulsive: F/3, repulsive.

Answer: option (4) — F/3, repulsive

TrapSharing 6 + 2 = 8 μC (ignoring the sign) gives 4 μC each and 4F/3; keeping “attractive” forgets that the charges now have the same sign.
Question 5Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
In the network shown, the charge on the 3 μF capacitor is
  1. 36 μC
  2. 72 μC
  3. 48 μC
  4. 24 μC
Concept to recallSeries: same charge, 1/C = 1/C1 + 1/C2. Parallel branches each have the full battery voltage.
  1. The 6 μF–3 μF branch is in series: C = 6 × 3/(6 + 3) = 2 μF.
  2. That branch has the full 12 V across it (it is in parallel with the 4 μF): Q = 2 × 12 = 24 μC.
  3. In series the 3 μF carries the same charge, 24 μC.

Answer: option (4) — 24 μC

Trap36 μC puts the whole 12 V across the 3 μF; 72 μC is the total charge drawn from the battery (Ceq = 6 μF); 48 μC is the charge on the 4 μF.
Question 6Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
A parallel-plate air capacitor is connected to a battery. With the battery still connected, the space between the plates is completely filled with a slab of dielectric constant 4. The ratio of the final to the initial energy stored in the capacitor is
  1. 1
  2. 4
  3. 1/4
  4. 16
Concept to recallBattery connected ⇒ V fixed, so use U = ½CV2. Battery disconnected ⇒ Q fixed, so use U = Q2/2C.
  1. The dielectric makes C → 4C.
  2. V is held by the battery, so U = ½CV2 becomes 4 times.

Answer: option (2) — 4

Trap1/4 is the result for an isolated capacitor (Q fixed); 16 squares K; 1 assumes the battery fixes the energy.
Question 7Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
An electric dipole is placed in a uniform electric field with its dipole moment making an angle θ (0 < θ < 180°) with the field. The dipole experiences
  1. a net force along E, and no torque
  2. zero net force, and a torque that tends to turn p opposite to E
  3. a net force along E, and a torque that tends to align p with E
  4. zero net force, and a torque that tends to align p with E
Concept to recallIn a uniform field the forces +qE and −qE cancel, but they act along different lines: τ = p × E, which turns p towards E (U = −pE cos θ is least at θ = 0).
  1. Net force = qE − qE = 0 in a uniform field.
  2. The couple has magnitude pE sin θ ≠ 0 and rotates p towards E, the stable orientation of lowest energy.

Answer: option (4) — zero net force, and a torque that tends to align p with E

TrapA net force on a dipole needs a non-uniform field; the torque never drives p towards the unstable θ = 180° position.
Question 8Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
The graph shows how the electric potential V varies along the x-axis. The electric field in the region 5 cm < x < 6 cm is
  1. zero
  2. 1500 V m−1 along +x
  3. 1500 V m−1 along −x
  4. 1000 V m−1 along +x
Concept to recallEx = −dV/dx: the field points the way V falls, and its size is the slope of the V–x graph.
  1. From x = 5 cm to 6 cm, V falls from +10 V to −5 V: ΔV = −15 V over Δx = 0.01 m.
  2. Ex = −(−15)/0.01 = +1500 V m−1, i.e. along +x.

Answer: option (2) — 1500 V m−1 along +x

Trap−x reads the slope’s sign without the minus in E = −dV/dx; 1000 V m−1 uses only the drop to zero (10 V); “zero” is the field in 2–5 cm, where V is constant.
Question 9Current Electricity
A copper wire of diameter 2 mm carries a current of 3.2 A. If copper has 8 × 1028 free electrons per m3, the drift speed of the electrons is about (e = 1.6 × 10−19 C)
  1. 2.5 × 10−4 m s−1
  2. 8.0 × 10−5 m s−1
  3. 2.0 × 10−5 m s−1
  4. 6.25 × 10−5 m s−1
Concept to recallI = neAvd, with A = πr2 = πd2/4.
  1. A = π(1 × 10−3)2 = 3.14 × 10−6 m2.
  2. vd = I/(neA) = 3.2/(8 × 1028 × 1.6 × 10−19 × 3.14 × 10−6) = 8.0 × 10−5 m s−1.

Answer: option (2) — 8.0 × 10−5 m s−1

Trap2.0 × 10−5 uses the diameter as the radius; 6.25 × 10−5 takes A = d2; 2.5 × 10−4 drops the π (A = r2).

In the bridge shown, the current drawn from the 6 V cell is
  1. 0.20 A
  2. 0.08 A
  3. 0.13 A
  4. 0.33 A
Concept to recallIf P/Q = R/S the bridge is balanced: no current in the galvanometer arm, which can then be removed.

Question 11Current Electricity
The terminal voltage of a cell is 5.6 V when it delivers 0.4 A and 5.0 V when it delivers 1.0 A. The emf and internal resistance of the cell are
  1. 6.2 V and 1.5 Ω
  2. 6.0 V and 1.0 Ω
  3. 6.0 V and 0.6 Ω
  4. 5.6 V and 1.0 Ω
Concept to recallV = ε − Ir: the terminal voltage falls linearly with current; r = −ΔV/ΔI.
  1. r = (5.6 − 5.0)/(1.0 − 0.4) = 0.6/0.6 = 1.0 Ω.
  2. ε = V + Ir = 5.6 + 0.4 × 1.0 = 6.0 V (check: 5.0 + 1.0 × 1.0 = 6.0 V).

Answer: option (2) — 6.0 V and 1.0 Ω

Trap0.6 Ω divides ΔV by 1.0 A instead of ΔI = 0.6 A; 5.6 V takes a terminal voltage as the emf; 6.2 V, 1.5 Ω divides ΔV by 0.4 A.

Question 13Current Electricity
Water in an electric kettle boils in 10 min when coil A alone is connected to the mains, and in 15 min when coil B alone is connected. With the two coils connected in parallel to the same mains, the same water boils in (neglect heat losses)
  1. 5 min
  2. 6 min
  3. 25 min
  4. 12.5 min
Concept to recallThe heat needed is fixed. On a fixed supply each coil delivers P = V2/R. In parallel both coils get the full V, so their powers add: 1/t = 1/tA + 1/tB. In series the resistances add instead, so t = tA + tB.
  1. Let Q be the heat needed: PA = Q/10 and PB = Q/15 (per minute).
  2. In parallel each coil still has the full mains voltage: P = PA + PB = Q(1/10 + 1/15) = Q/6.
  3. t = Q/P = 6 min.

Answer: option (2) — 6 min

Trap25 min is the series answer (RA + RB, so the times add); 12.5 min averages the two times; 5 min subtracts them.
Question 14Current Electricity
Consider the following statements:
  1. (A) The resistivity of a metallic conductor increases with temperature.
  2. (B) The resistivity of an intrinsic semiconductor decreases as temperature rises.
  3. (C) Alloys such as manganin have a very small temperature coefficient of resistivity.
  4. (D) In a metal, the relaxation time of the free electrons increases with temperature.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (B), (C) and (D) only
  2. (A), (B), (C) and (D)
  3. (A), (B) and (C) only
  4. (A) and (D) only
Concept to recallρ = m/(ne2τ). Metals: n fixed, τ falls as T rises, so ρ rises. Semiconductors: n rises steeply with T, so ρ falls.
  1. (A) true: more vigorous lattice vibrations ⇒ more frequent collisions.
  2. (B) true: more carriers are freed across the band gap.
  3. (C) true: that is why manganin and constantan are used for standard resistors.
  4. (D) false: collisions are more frequent, so τ decreases.

Answer: option (3) — (A), (B) and (C) only

Trap(D) reverses the cause: ρ rises in a metal precisely because τ falls.
Question 15Moving Charges and Magnetism
A proton, a deuteron and an α-particle with the same kinetic energy enter a uniform magnetic field at right angles to it. The ratio of the radii of their circular paths rp : rd : rα is
  1. 1 : √2 : 2
  2. 1 : 1 : ½
  3. 1 : √2 : 1
  4. 1 : 2 : 2
Concept to recallr = mv/(qB) = p/(qB) = √(2mK)/(qB). At equal K, r ∝ √m/q.
  1. Masses 1 : 2 : 4 (units of mp); charges 1 : 1 : 2 (units of e).
  2. √m/q: proton √1/1 = 1; deuteron √2/1 = √2; α √4/2 = 1.
  3. rp : rd : rα = 1 : √2 : 1.

Answer: option (3) — 1 : √2 : 1

Trap1 : 2 : 2 is the ratio at equal speed (r ∝ m/q); 1 : 1 : ½ at equal momentum; 1 : √2 : 2 forgets the α-particle’s double charge.

Question 18Moving Charges and Magnetism
A long wire carrying 10 A is bent into the hairpin shown: two semi-infinite straight parts joined by a semicircle of radius 5 cm centred at O. The magnetic field at O is about (μ0 = 4π × 10−7 T m A−1)
  1. 6.28 × 10−5 T
  2. 8.28 × 10−5 T
  3. 1.66 × 10−4 T
  4. 1.03 × 10−4 T
Concept to recallSemicircle at its centre: μ0I/(4R). Semi-infinite wire, at a point a distance R from the line opposite its end: μ0I/(4πR). All three parts give B into the page here.
  1. Semicircle: μ0I/(4R) = 4π × 10−7 × 10/(4 × 0.05) = 6.28 × 10−5 T.
  2. Each straight part: μ0I/(4πR) = 10−7 × 10/0.05 = 2 × 10−5 T; two of them give 4 × 10−5 T.

Answer: option (4) — 1.03 × 10−4 T

Trap6.28 × 10−5 T ignores the straight parts; 8.28 × 10−5 T counts only one of them; 1.66 × 10−4 T uses the full-circle μ0I/2R for a semicircle.
Question 19Moving Charges and Magnetism
A long solenoid of length 60 cm is wound in 3 layers of 300 turns each and carries a current of 2 A. The magnetic field well inside it, near its centre, is (μ0 = 4π × 10−7 T m A−1)
  1. 1.26 × 10−3 T
  2. 1.88 × 10−3 T
  3. 2.26 × 10−3 T
  4. 3.77 × 10−3 T
Concept to recallAmpère’s law round a rectangle with one side inside the solenoid: B = μ0nI, n = total turns per unit length. Every layer carries the same current, so the turns of all layers count; the radius does not enter.
  1. Total turns N = 3 × 300 = 900; n = 900/0.60 m = 1500 m−1.
  2. B = μ0nI = 4π × 10−7 × 1500 × 2 = 3.77 × 10−3 T.

Answer: option (4) — 3.77 × 10−3 T

Trap1.26 × 10−3 T counts one layer only; 1.88 × 10−3 T is μ0nI/2, the field at an end of the solenoid; 2.26 × 10−3 T is μ0NI, which forgets to divide the turns by the length.
Question 20Magnetism and Matter
A short solenoid of 400 turns, each of area 5 cm2, carries a current of 2 A. Treating it as a bar magnet, the magnetic field it produces at a point on its axis 20 cm from its centre is (μ0/4π = 10−7 T m A−1; the solenoid is small compared with 20 cm)
  1. 2.0 × 10−6 T
  2. 2.5 × 10−8 T
  3. 1.0 × 10−5 T
  4. 5.0 × 10−6 T
Concept to recallA current-carrying solenoid behaves as a bar magnet of moment m = NIA. Far from it the field is a dipole field: on the axis B = (μ0/4π)(2m/r3); on the perpendicular bisector B = (μ0/4π)(m/r3).
  1. m = NIA = 400 × 2 × 5 × 10−4 = 0.4 A m2.
  2. Axial point: B = 10−7 × 2 × 0.4/(0.2)3 = 8 × 10−8/(8 × 10−3) = 1.0 × 10−5 T.

Answer: option (3) — 1.0 × 10−5 T

Trap5.0 × 10−6 T is the field on the perpendicular bisector (no factor 2); 2.0 × 10−6 T uses r2 in place of r3; 2.5 × 10−8 T leaves out N, taking m = IA for a single turn.

Question 23Electromagnetic Induction
The current through a 0.2 H inductor varies with time as shown. The largest magnitude of the self-induced emf, and the interval in which it occurs, are
  1. 0.8 V, during 2 s to 4 s
  2. 0.16 V, during 4 s to 5 s
  3. 0.8 V, during 4 s to 5 s
  4. 0.4 V, during 0 to 2 s
Concept to recallε = −L dI/dt: the emf follows the slope of the I–t graph, not the height.
  1. 0–2 s: dI/dt = 4/2 = 2 A s−1 ⇒ |ε| = 0.2 × 2 = 0.4 V.

Question 24Electromagnetic Induction
Consider the following statements about electromagnetic induction:
  1. (A) Lenz’s law is a consequence of the law of conservation of energy.
  2. (B) The mutual inductance of two coils depends on their sizes, numbers of turns and relative position, not on the currents in them.
  3. (C) The emf induced in a coil depends on the magnetic flux linked with it, not on how fast the flux changes.
  4. (D) For a given length and cross-section, the self-inductance of a long solenoid is proportional to the square of its number of turns per unit length.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (A), (B) and (D) only
  2. (A), (B) and (C) only
  3. (B) and (D) only
  4. (A) and (C) only
Concept to recallFaraday: ε = −dΦ/dt (rate of change). Lenz: the induced current opposes the change that causes it – otherwise energy would be created. Inductance is fixed by geometry (and the core): L = μ0n2Al for a solenoid, M = μ0n1n2πr12l for two coaxial solenoids.
  1. (A) true: if the induced current aided the change, energy would grow with no work done.
  2. (B) true: M depends only on the geometry, e.g. M = μ0n1n2πr12l; the current appears on both sides of Φ = MI and cancels.
  3. (C) false: a large steady flux induces nothing; only a changing flux does.
  4. (D) true: L = μ0n2Al.

Answer: option (1) — (A), (B) and (D) only

Trap“(A), (B) and (C)” confuses flux with its rate of change and drops the n2 law; “(B) and (D) only” misses that Lenz’s law is energy conservation; “(A) and (C) only” makes both slips and also thinks inductance depends on the current.

  1. 640 W

Question 26Alternating Current
In an ac circuit the voltage is v = V0 sin ωt and the current is i = I0 sin(ωt + π/2). The circuit contains
