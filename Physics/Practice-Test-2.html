Question 1Electric Charges and Fields
Three equal charges of +1 μC each sit at the corners of an equilateral triangle of side 10 cm. The magnitude of the net electrostatic force on any one of them is (k = 9 × 109 N m2 C−2)
  1. 0.90 N
  2. zero, by symmetry
  3. 1.56 N
  4. 1.80 N
Concept to recallSuperposition: add the forces as vectors. Two equal forces F at angle θ give 2F cos(θ/2); here θ = 60°, so the resultant is √3 F.
  1. Each pair: F = kq2/a2 = 9 × 109 × (10−6)2 / (0.1)2 = 0.9 N.
  2. The two repulsions on one charge make 60° with each other.
  3. Resultant = 2F cos 30° = √3 × 0.9 = 1.56 N, along the bisector, away from the triangle.

Answer: option (3) — 1.56 N

Trap1.80 N adds the two forces as if parallel; 0.90 N counts only one neighbour; “zero” confuses the force on a vertex charge with the field at the centre, which is zero.
Question 2Electric Charges and Fields
A small sphere of mass 1 g carrying a charge of 2 × 10−8 C hangs from a silk thread near a large, vertical, uniformly charged non-conducting sheet. In equilibrium the thread makes 45° with the vertical. The surface charge density of the sheet is (g = 9.8 m s−2, ε0 = 8.85 × 10−12 C2 N−1 m−2)
  1. 8.7 × 10−6 C m−2
  2. 4.3 × 10−6 C m−2
  3. 8.7 × 10−3 C m−2
  4. 6.1 × 10−6 C m−2
Concept to recallField of an infinite sheet E = σ/2ε0 (uniform, normal to the sheet). Hanging charge: tan θ = qE/mg.
  1. tan 45° = 1 ⇒ qE = mg ⇒ qσ/2ε0 = mg.
  2. σ = 2ε0mg/q = 2 × 8.85 × 10−12 × 10−3 × 9.8 / 2 × 10−8 = 8.7 × 10−6 C m−2.

Answer: option (1) — 8.7 × 10−6 C m−2

Trap4.3 × 10−6 uses the conductor field σ/ε0; 8.7 × 10−3 leaves the mass in grams; 6.1 × 10−6 balances the electric force against mg sin 45° instead of using tan θ.
Question 3Electric Charges and Fields
An electric dipole of moment 4 × 10−9 C m is held in a uniform field of 5 × 104 N C−1 with its axis at 30° to the field. The work needed to turn it slowly until it is perpendicular to the field is
  1. 1.0 × 10−4 J
  2. 2.0 × 10−4 J
  3. 2.7 × 10−5 J
  4. 1.73 × 10−4 J
Concept to recallU(θ) = −pE cos θ, so W = U2 − U1 = pE (cos θ1 − cos θ2).
  1. pE = 4 × 10−9 × 5 × 104 = 2 × 10−4 J.
  2. W = pE (cos 30° − cos 90°) = 2 × 10−4 × 0.866 = 1.73 × 10−4 J.

Answer: option (4) — 1.73 × 10−4 J

Trap1.0 × 10−4 J uses sines (it is also the torque at 30°, in N m); 2.0 × 10−4 J starts from θ = 0; 2.7 × 10−5 J is pE(1 − cos 30°), the work from 0° to 30°.
Question 4Electric Charges and Fields
Consider the following statements about electrostatics.
  1. (A) The field inside an empty cavity in a conductor is zero, whatever the charges outside.
  2. (B) Inside the material of a charged conductor in electrostatic equilibrium the field is zero.
  3. (C) Just outside a charged conductor the field is σ/2ε0.
  4. (D) The total flux through a closed surface depends on charges lying outside it.
  5. (E) The field of an infinite uniformly charged plane sheet does not depend on the distance from it.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (A), (C) and (D) only
  2. (A), (B) and (E) only
  3. (A), (B) and (C) only
  4. (B), (D) and (E) only
Concept to recallConductor: E = 0 inside the material and in an empty cavity (electrostatic shielding), E = σ/ε0 just outside. Gauss: flux depends only on the enclosed charge. Sheet: E = σ/2ε0, uniform.
  1. (A) true — electrostatic shielding.
  2. (B) true — otherwise free charges would keep moving.
  3. (C) false — σ/2ε0 is the sheet; for a conductor it is σ/ε0.
  4. (D) false — outside charges change the field on the surface, not the net flux.
  5. (E) true — E = σ/2ε0.

Answer: option (2) — (A), (B) and (E) only

Trap(C) swaps the conductor and sheet results; (D) confuses the field on a Gaussian surface with the flux through it.
Question 5Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
A parallel-plate air capacitor has capacitance 6 μF. A slab of dielectric constant 4, of thickness half the plate separation and covering the whole plate area, is inserted as shown. The new capacitance is
  1. 2.4 μF
  2. 9.6 μF
  3. 15 μF
  4. 24 μF
Concept to recallA slab of thickness t across the full area: C = ε0A / (d − t + t/K) — the air layer and the slab are two capacitors in series.
  1. With t = d/2: C = ε0A / (d/2 + d/8) = ε0A / (5d/8) = (8/5) C0.
  2. C = 1.6 × 6 = 9.6 μF.
  3. Check as series: 2C0 = 12 μF (air half) and 2KC0 = 48 μF (slab half): 12 × 48 / 60 = 9.6 μF.

Answer: option (2) — 9.6 μF

Trap15 μF treats the halves as side by side (parallel), (1 + K)C0/2; 24 μF fills the whole gap (KC0); 2.4 μF multiplies by K where it should divide (d/2 + Kd/2).
Question 6Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
A 3 μF capacitor charged to 60 V is connected across an uncharged 6 μF capacitor. The energy lost as heat (and radiation) in the redistribution is
  1. 1.8 mJ
  2. 5.4 mJ
  3. zero, since energy is conserved
  4. 3.6 mJ
Concept to recallCharge is conserved: V = C1V1/(C1 + C2). Energy is not: ΔU = C1C2V12 / 2(C1 + C2).
  1. Q = 3 μF × 60 V = 180 μC; common V = 180/9 = 20 V.
  2. Ui = ½ (3 × 10−6)(60)2 = 5.4 mJ; Uf = ½ (9 × 10−6)(20)2 = 1.8 mJ.
  3. Loss = 3.6 mJ. (Formula: 3 × 6 × 3600 / 18 μJ = 3600 μJ.)

Answer: option (4) — 3.6 mJ

Trap1.8 mJ is the energy left, not the energy lost; 5.4 mJ is the initial energy; “zero” forgets the resistance of the joining wires dissipates energy.
Question 7Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
Charges +2 μC, +2 μC, −2 μC and +2 μC are placed at the four corners of a square of side 20 cm. The electric potential at the centre of the square is (k = 9 × 109 N m2 C−2)
  1. 2.55 × 105 V
  2. 3.6 × 105 V
  3. 5.09 × 105 V
  4. 1.8 × 105 V
Concept to recallPotential is a scalar: V = kΣqi/ri, signs included. Each corner is half a diagonal, a/√2, from the centre.
  1. Σq = 2 + 2 − 2 + 2 = 4 μC.
  2. r = 0.20/√2 = 0.1414 m.
  3. V = 9 × 109 × 4 × 10−6 / 0.1414 = 2.55 × 105 V.

Answer: option (1) — 2.55 × 105 V

Trap3.6 × 105 V uses r = a/2 (half a side); 5.09 × 105 V adds the magnitudes, ignoring the minus sign; 1.8 × 105 V uses r = a.
Question 8Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
The graph shows the x-component of the electric field along the x-axis. The potential difference V(0) − V(x = 8 cm) is
  1. 16 V
  2. 2400 V
  3. 24 V
  4. 32 V

  2. 4–8 cm (rectangle): 0.04 m × 400 V/m = 16 V.

Question 9Current Electricity
Two cells, E1 = 12 V with internal resistance 2 Ω and E2 = 6 V with internal resistance 1 Ω, are connected in parallel (positive terminals together) across an external resistor R = 6 Ω. The current through R is
  1. 2.0 A
  2. 0.67 A
  3. 1.5 A
  4. 1.2 A
Concept to recallKirchhoff’s junction and loop rules; for cells in parallel the common terminal voltage is V = (E1/r1 + E2/r2) / (1/r1 + 1/r2 + 1/R).
  1. Let V be the potential of P above Q. Junction rule at P: (12 − V)/2 + (6 − V)/1 = V/6.
  2. Multiply by 6: 36 − 3V + 36 − 6V = V ⇒ 10V = 72 ⇒ V = 7.2 V.
  3. IR = 7.2/6 = 1.2 A. (Cell 2 carries (6 − 7.2)/1 = −1.2 A: it is being charged.)

Answer: option (4) — 1.2 A

Trap2.0 A puts the cells in series aiding (18 V / 9 Ω); 0.67 A in series opposing (6 V / 9 Ω); 1.5 A averages the emfs (9 V / 6 Ω).
Question 10Current Electricity
Ten identical cells, each of emf 1.5 V and internal resistance 0.5 Ω, are meant to be joined in series, but two of them are connected the wrong way round. The battery is connected across an external resistance of 4 Ω. The current in the circuit is
  1. 1.13 A
  2. 1.0 A
  3. 1.33 A
  4. 2.25 A
Concept to recallA reversed cell subtracts its emf instead of adding it, but its internal resistance is still in the series path. With m of n cells reversed: Enet = (n − 2m)E and rnet = nr, so I = Enet/(R + nr).
  1. Each reversed cell cancels one correctly joined cell as well as its own contribution: Enet = (10 − 2 × 2) × 1.5 = 9 V.
  2. All ten internal resistances are in series: r = 10 × 0.5 = 5 Ω.
  3. I = 9/(4 + 5) = 1.0 A.

Answer: option (2) — 1.0 A

Trap1.33 A only leaves the two reversed cells out (8 × 1.5 = 12 V) instead of subtracting them; 2.25 A ignores the internal resistances; 1.13 A drops the internal resistance of the two reversed cells.
Question 11Current Electricity
In a Wheatstone bridge the four arms are P = 10 Ω, Q = 10 Ω, R = 10 Ω and S = 15 Ω, the bridge being balanced when P/Q = R/S. To balance it, the resistance that must be connected in parallel with S is
  1. 6 Ω
  2. 25 Ω
  3. 30 Ω
  4. 5 Ω
Concept to recallA Wheatstone bridge is balanced, with no current through the galvanometer, when P/Q = R/S. A resistor X in parallel with S lowers it to S′ given by 1/S′ = 1/S + 1/X.
  1. Balance needs S′ = QR/P = 10 × 10/10 = 10 Ω.
  2. The 15 Ω arm must be brought down to 10 Ω: 1/X = 1/10 − 1/15 = 1/30.
  3. X = 30 Ω.

Answer: option (3) — 30 Ω

Trap5 Ω is the difference 15 − 10, the series thinking (and a series resistor can only raise S); 6 Ω is 15 Ω and 10 Ω in parallel, i.e. the target value put in parallel with S; 25 Ω adds them.
Question 12Current Electricity
A metal wire is connected across a battery of constant emf (negligible internal resistance). It is then stretched uniformly to twice its length and reconnected to the same battery. The drift speed of its free electrons becomes
  1. half the original
  2. unchanged
  3. twice the original
  4. one-quarter of the original
Concept to recallvd = eEτ/m with E = V/l: at fixed V, the drift speed depends only on the length, not on the cross-section.
  1. Stretched to 2l (volume fixed), the field becomes E′ = V/2l = E/2.
  2. τ and n are properties of the metal and do not change.
  3. So vd′ = vd/2.
  4. (Check by I = neAvd: R becomes 4R so I → I/4, A → A/2, so vd → (1/4)/(1/2) = 1/2.)

Answer: option (1) — half the original

Trap“One-quarter” follows the current (R becomes 4R) but forgets the area halved too.
Question 13Current Electricity
A battery of emf 10 V and internal resistance 2 Ω delivers the same power to an external resistor of 8 Ω as it does to another external resistor R. The value of R is
  1. 0.5 Ω
  2. 2 Ω
  3. 4 Ω
  4. 6 Ω
Concept to recallP = E2R/(R + r)2 takes each value twice; the two loads that give equal power satisfy R1R2 = r2.
  1. Power in 8 Ω: 100 × 8 / 100 = 8 W.
  2. Equal power: R1R2 = r2 ⇒ 8R = 4 ⇒ R = 0.5 Ω.
  3. Check: 100 × 0.5 / 2.52 = 50/6.25 = 8 W.

Answer: option (1) — 0.5 Ω

Trap2 Ω is the maximum-power load (R = r), not the partner; 4 Ω is the geometric mean √(8 × 2); 6 Ω subtracts r from 8 Ω.
Question 14Current Electricity
The V–I graphs of two wires A and B of the same material and the same length are straight lines through the origin making 60° and 30° with the I-axis. The ratio of their cross-sectional areas, AA : AB, is
  1. 1 : √3
  2. 2 : 1
  3. 1 : 3
  4. 3 : 1
Concept to recallOn a V–I graph the slope is R = V/I = tan θ. With ρ and l the same, R = ρl/A, so A ∝ 1/R.
  1. RA/RB = tan 60° / tan 30° = √3 / (1/√3) = 3.
  2. AA/AB = RB/RA = 1/3.

Answer: option (3) — 1 : 3

Trap3 : 1 is the ratio of resistances; 1 : √3 is the ratio of diameters; 2 : 1 is the ratio of the angles.
Question 15Moving Charges and Magnetism
A proton passes undeflected through a region with crossed fields E = 3 × 104 V/m and B = 0.02 T. The electric field is now switched off. The radius of the proton’s path is (mp = 1.67 × 10−27 kg, e = 1.6 × 10−19 C)

  2. 0.78 m

  4. 3.1 × 10−4 m
Concept to recallVelocity selector: qE = qvB ⇒ v = E/B. In B alone: r = mv/(qB).
  1. v = E/B = 3 × 104/0.02 = 1.5 × 106 m/s.
  2. r = (1.67 × 10−27 × 1.5 × 106) / (1.6 × 10−19 × 0.02) = 0.78 m.

Question 16Moving Charges and Magnetism
Two long parallel wires 10 cm apart carry currents of 5 A and 8 A in opposite directions. The force per unit length on each wire is (μ0 = 4π × 10−7 T m/A)
  1. 8 × 10−5 N/m, attractive
  2. 4 × 10−5 N/m, repulsive
  3. 8 × 10−7 N/m, repulsive
  4. 8 × 10−5 N/m, repulsive
Concept to recallF/l = μ0I1I2/(2πd); like currents attract, unlike currents repel.

Question 17Moving Charges and Magnetism
A long wire carrying 6 A is bent as shown: two straight parts along a diameter line and a semicircle of radius 4 cm between them. The magnetic field at the centre O is (μ0 = 4π × 10−7 T m/A)
  1. 2.36 × 10−5 T
  2. 3.0 × 10−5 T
  3. 4.71 × 10−5 T
  4. 9.42 × 10−5 T
Concept to recallBiot–Savart: a straight segment whose line passes through the point (dl ∥ r) gives zero; a circular arc of angle θ gives μ0Iθ/(4πR).
  1. Straight parts: dl × r = 0, so no field at O.
  2. Semicircle (θ = π): B = μ0I/(4R) = 4π × 10−7 × 6 / (4 × 0.04) = 4.71 × 10−5 T.

Answer: option (3) — 4.71 × 10−5 T

Trap9.42 × 10−5 T is a full loop (μ0I/2R); 2.36 × 10−5 T is a quarter circle; 3.0 × 10−5 T is a long straight wire at distance R (μ0I/2πR).

Question 19Moving Charges and Magnetism

Question 21Magnetism and Matter
A paramagnetic sample at 300 K, in an external magnetic field of 0.3 T, has a magnetisation of 6 A m−1. It is cooled to 150 K and the field is raised to 0.5 T. Its magnetisation now is (the sample is far from saturation)
  1. 5 A m−1
  2. 12 A m−1
  3. 10 A m−1
  4. 20 A m−1
Concept to recallCurie’s law for a paramagnet: χ = C/T. With M = χH and H = B/μ0, M = CB/(μ0T) ∝ B/T (valid far from saturation).
  1. M2/M1 = (B2/B1)(T1/T2) = (0.5/0.3)(300/150) = 10/3.
  2. M2 = 6 × 10/3 = 20 A m−1.

Trap5 A m−1 takes M ∝ T, as if heating helped alignment; 12 A m−1 uses only the temperature change; 10 A m−1 uses only the field change.

Question 25Alternating Current
A series LCR circuit with R = 40 Ω, XL = 60 Ω and XC = 30 Ω is connected to a 250 V (rms) supply and dissipates power P1. The supply frequency is then changed (rms voltage unchanged) until the circuit is at resonance, when it dissipates P2. The ratio P2/P1 is
  1. 1.00
  2. 1.56
  3. 1.25
  4. 0.64
Concept to recallP = Irms2R = V2R/Z2 = (V2/R) cos2φ. At resonance Z = R, cos φ = 1 and P is the largest possible, V2/R.
  1. Z = √(402 + 302) = 50 Ω; I = 5 A; P1 = 25 × 40 = 1000 W (cos φ = 0.8).
  2. At resonance: P2 = 2502/40 = 1562.5 W.
  3. P2/P1 = 1/cos2φ = 1/0.64 = 1.56.

Answer: option (2) — 1.56

Trap1.25 is 1/cos φ (the power factor not squared); 0.64 is P1/P2; 1.00 assumes the power depends only on R, which has not changed.
Question 26Alternating Current

  3. 2500 V
