Question 1Electric Charges and Fields
A point charge +q is placed at the centre of an uncharged hollow conducting sphere. The charges induced on the inner and the outer surface of the sphere are respectively
  1. +q and −q
  2. −q and zero
  3. −q and +q
  4. zero and zero
Concept to recallInside the material of a conductor E = 0 in equilibrium. A Gaussian surface drawn inside the metal must therefore enclose zero net charge; the conductor as a whole stays neutral.
  1. Gaussian surface inside the metal: q + qinner = 0, so qinner = −q.
  2. The sphere was neutral: qouter = 0 − (−q) = +q.

Answer: option (3) — −q and +q

Trap−q and zero forgets that the sphere must stay neutral; a conductor shields its cavity from outside charges, not the outside from a charge in the cavity.
Question 2Electric Charges and Fields
Two point charges repel each other with a force F. If each charge is doubled and the distance between them is halved, the force becomes
  1. 16 F
  2. 4 F
  3. 8 F
  4. F
Concept to recallCoulomb’s law: F ∝ q1q2/r2.
  1. Charges: 2 × 2 = 4 times.
  2. Distance halved: 1/(1/2)2 = 4 times.
  3. New force = 4 × 4 F = 16 F.

Answer: option (1) — 16 F

Trap4F forgets the distance; 8F doubles only one charge; F assumes the changes cancel.
Question 3Electric Charges and Fields
Consider the statements about fields of extended charge distributions (σ = surface charge density, λ = linear charge density):
  1. (A) The field of a very large plane sheet of charge is σ/(2ε0) and does not depend on the distance from the sheet.
  2. (B) Between two very large parallel sheets carrying +σ and −σ the field is σ/ε0.
  3. (C) Outside those two oppositely charged sheets the field is also σ/ε0.
  4. (D) The field of a very long straight line charge falls off as 1/r.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (A), (B), (C) and (D)
  2. (A), (B) and (D) only
  3. (B) and (D) only
  4. (A) and (C) only
Concept to recallGauss’s law: sheet (pill-box) E = σ/2ε0; line (cylinder) E = λ/(2πε0r). Fields of several sheets add as vectors.
  1. (A) true: the pill-box result has no r in it.
  2. (B) true: between the sheets the two fields σ/2ε0 point the same way and add.
  3. (C) false: outside they point opposite ways and cancel; the field is zero.
  4. (D) true: E = λ/(2πε0r).

Answer: option (2) — (A), (B) and (D) only

TrapThe parallel-plate capacitor works because the field is confined between the plates; outside it is zero.
Question 4Electric Charges and Fields
The graph shows the magnitude of the electric field E against the distance r from the centre of a spherically symmetric charge distribution of radius R. The distribution is
  1. a uniformly charged non-conducting solid sphere
  2. a point charge placed at the centre
  3. two concentric thin shells carrying equal and opposite charges
  4. a uniformly charged thin spherical shell
Concept to recallGauss’s law with a spherical Gaussian surface: E · 4πr2 = qenc/ε0.
  1. For r < R the graph gives E = 0, so no charge lies inside radius R: all of it is on the surface.
  2. For r ≥ R, E = kq/r2, as for a point charge.
  3. Zero inside, a jump at R, then 1/r2: a thin shell (or a charged conductor).

Answer: option (4) — a uniformly charged thin spherical shell

TrapA solid non-conducting sphere gives E rising linearly from zero inside; a point charge has no zero region; equal and opposite shells give zero field outside.

Question 7Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

  1. 0.9 J
  2. 0.3 J
  3. 0.6 J
  4. 1.2 J
Concept to recallWext = ΔU = kQq(1/rf − 1/ri).
  1. kQq = 9 × 109 × 5 × 10−6 × 2 × 10−6 = 0.09 J m.
  2. 1/0.1 − 1/0.3 = 10 − 3.33 = 6.67 m−1.

Question 9Current Electricity
A wire of resistance 5 Ω is drawn out uniformly until its radius is halved. Its new resistance is
  1. 20 Ω
  2. 10 Ω
  3. 80 Ω
  4. 40 Ω
Concept to recallVolume is constant: Al = constant. R = ρl/A ∝ 1/A2 ∝ 1/r4.
  1. Radius halved: A becomes A/4, so l becomes 4l.
  2. R ∝ l/A: 4 × 4 = 16 times. New R = 16 × 5 = 80 Ω.

Answer: option (3) — 80 Ω

Trap20 Ω counts only the smaller area (or only the longer length); 40 Ω uses a factor of 8.
Question 10Current Electricity
A cell of emf 12 V and internal resistance 1 Ω is connected across an external resistance of 5 Ω. The potential difference across the terminals of the cell is
  1. 12 V
  2. 10 V
  3. 2 V
  4. 14 V
Concept to recallI = ε/(R + r); terminal voltage V = ε − Ir (discharging).
  1. I = 12/(5 + 1) = 2 A.
  2. V = 12 − 2 × 1 = 10 V (= IR = 2 × 5).

Answer: option (2) — 10 V

Trap12 V ignores the internal drop; 2 V is the drop across r; 14 V is ε + Ir, which holds only while the cell is being charged.
Question 11Current Electricity
A steady current flows through a metal wire whose cross-section decreases steadily from end P to end Q. As one moves from P to Q:
  1. (A) the current stays the same
  2. (B) the current density increases
  3. (C) the drift speed of the free electrons decreases
  4. (D) the electric field inside the wire increases
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (A), (B) and (D) only
  2. (A), (B) and (C) only
  3. (A), (B), (C) and (D)
  4. (A) only
Concept to recallSteady current: I is the same through every section (charge conservation). j = I/A = nevd and j = σE.
  1. (A) true: no charge piles up anywhere.
  2. (B) true: j = I/A grows as A shrinks.
  3. (C) false: vd = j/(ne) also grows.
  4. (D) true: E = j/σ grows.

Answer: option (1) — (A), (B) and (D) only

TrapIn the narrow part the electrons go faster, not slower, just as water does in a narrow pipe.
Question 12Current Electricity
The V–I graphs of the same metallic wire at two temperatures T1 and T2 are shown. Which is correct?
  1. T1 < T2
  2. T1 = T2, since the wire obeys Ohm’s law
  3. It cannot be decided without the length of the wire
  4. T1 > T2
Concept to recallSlope of a V–I graph = R. For a metal, R rises with temperature: R = R0(1 + αΔT).
  1. Line T1 is steeper, so R1 > R2.
  2. Higher resistance of a metal means higher temperature: T1 > T2.

Answer: option (4) — T1 > T2

TrapOn an I–V graph (current on the y-axis) the slope is 1/R and the conclusion reverses. Check which quantity is on which axis.
Question 13Current Electricity
In the circuit shown, a cell of emf 12 V and internal resistance 0.5 Ω is connected to a 1.5 Ω resistor in series with a parallel combination of 6 Ω and 12 Ω. The current through the 12 Ω resistor is
  1. 2.0 A
  2. 0.67 A
  3. 1.33 A
  4. 1.0 A
Concept to recallReduce the network: parallel Rp = R1R2/(R1 + R2); I = ε/(Rext + r); current divides inversely as the resistances.
  1. 6 Ω ∥ 12 Ω = 4 Ω; total = 4 + 1.5 + 0.5 = 6 Ω; I = 12/6 = 2 A.

Question 14Current Electricity
Two bulbs rated 100 W, 220 V and 60 W, 220 V are connected in series across a 220 V supply. Then
  1. the 100 W bulb glows brighter
  2. both glow equally, since the same current flows
  3. the 60 W bulb glows brighter
  4. the 100 W bulb glows brighter, as it does in parallel
Concept to recallR = Vrated2/Prated. In series the current is common, so power I2R is larger in the larger resistance.
  1. R60 = 2202/60 > R100 = 2202/100.

TrapThe rating is the power at 220 V across the bulb; in series neither bulb gets 220 V.
Question 15Moving Charges and Magnetism
An electron moves along the +x direction in a uniform magnetic field directed along +z. The magnetic force on the electron is along
  1. −y
  2. +z
  3. −x
  4. +y
Concept to recallLorentz force F = q(v × B); for an electron q = −e.
  1. v × B ∝ x̂ × ẑ = −ŷ.
  2. Multiply by −e: F is along +y.

Answer: option (4) — +y

Trap−y is the force on a positive charge; the magnetic force is never along B or along v.
Question 16Moving Charges and Magnetism
A charged particle moves on a circle at right angles to a uniform magnetic field. If its kinetic energy is doubled, the radius of the circle and the period of revolution become
  1. 2 times, and unchanged
  2. √2 times, and unchanged
  3. √2 times, and √2 times
  4. 2 times, and half
Concept to recallr = mv/(qB) = √(2mK)/(qB) ∝ √K; T = 2πm/(qB), independent of speed.
  1. K doubled: v grows by √2, and so does r.
  2. The path grows in proportion to the speed, so the period does not change.

Answer: option (2) — √2 times, and unchanged

TrapDoubling K does not double v; and a faster particle on a larger circle takes the same time.

Question 19Moving Charges and Magnetism
A charged particle enters a uniform magnetic field with its velocity at 60° to the field and moves on a helix. The ratio of the pitch of the helix to its radius is
  1. 2π√3 (≈ 10.9)
  2. 2π (≈ 6.28)
  3. 2π/√3 (≈ 3.63)
  4. π/√3 (≈ 1.81)
Concept to recallSplit v: v sin θ (perpendicular to B) sets the circle, r = mv sin θ/(qB); v cos θ (along B) carries it forward one pitch per period T = 2πm/(qB).

  2. p/r = 2π cos θ/sin θ = 2π cot 60° = 2π/√3.

Answer: option (3) — 2π/√3 (≈ 3.63)

Trap2π√3 swaps sin and cos (it would be right for 30°); 2π forgets the angle.

Question 21Magnetism and Matter
Consider the statements about magnetic materials:
  1. (A) Ferromagnetism arises from domains in which the atomic magnetic moments are aligned.
  2. (B) Above its Curie temperature a ferromagnet becomes paramagnetic.
  3. (C) The susceptibility of a paramagnetic material increases as its temperature rises.
  4. (D) The susceptibility of a diamagnetic material is small and negative, and nearly independent of temperature.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (A), (B), (C) and (D)
  2. (A) and (D) only
  3. (A), (B) and (D) only
  4. (B) and (C) only
Concept to recallCurie’s law for paramagnets: χ = C/T, so thermal agitation lowers χ. Above TC a ferromagnet’s domains break up and it obeys χ ∝ 1/(T − TC).

Question 25Alternating Current
Two series LCR circuits with the same L and C but resistances R1 and R2 are driven by sources of the same voltage amplitude. Their current amplitude against angular frequency is shown as curves 1 and 2. Which is correct?
  1. R1 > R2, and both resonate at the same ω0
  2. R1 < R2, and circuit 2 resonates at a lower frequency
  3. R1 < R2, and both resonate at the same ω0
  4. R1 > R2, and circuit 1 has the lower quality factor
Concept to recallAt resonance XL = XC, ω0 = 1/√(LC) (independent of R), and Imax = V0/R. Q = ω0L/R: smaller R, sharper peak.

Answer: option (3) — R1 < R2, and both resonate at the same ω0

TrapResistance lowers and broadens the peak but does not move it.

Question 27Alternating Current
In an ideal step-down transformer, which of the following is the same in the primary and the secondary?
  1. the rms voltage
  2. the frequency of the alternating voltage
  3. the rms current
  4. the flux linkage NΦ
Concept to recallThe same changing flux Φ threads every turn of both coils, so the emf per turn and the frequency match; Vs/Vp = Ns/Np = Ip/Is.

Question 28Electromagnetic Waves

  2. their frequency
  3. their wavelength
  4. the energy of their photons
Concept to recallAll electromagnetic waves travel in vacuum at c = 1/√(μ0ε0) = 3 × 108 m s−1; they differ in ν, λ = c/ν and photon energy hν.

  2. (A), (B) and (C) only
  3. (B) and (D) only
  4. (A), (B) and (D) only

  2. 2 : 1
  3. 4 : 1
  4. 5 : 4

Question 43Atoms

  1. most of the volume of an atom is empty space
  2. the positive charge is spread uniformly through the atom
  3. electrons are distributed throughout the atom
  4. the nucleus carries a negative charge

Question 44Nuclei
Energy is released when a heavy nucleus such as 235U splits into two middle-mass nuclei because
  1. the products have a smaller binding energy per nucleon
  2. the total number of nucleons decreases
  3. the products have a greater total mass than the parent
  4. the products have a greater binding energy per nucleon
Concept to recallThe binding-energy-per-nucleon curve peaks near A ≈ 56 (about 8.8 MeV) and falls to about 7.6 MeV at A = 238. Moving towards the peak (fission of heavy, fusion of light nuclei) releases energy.
  1. Splitting A ≈ 236 into two nuclei near A ≈ 120 raises B/A by about 0.9 MeV.

TrapNucleon number is conserved; it is the binding that changes. Greater product mass would absorb energy.

  4. a p-type semiconductor
