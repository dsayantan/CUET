Question 1Solutions
3.0 g of urea (NH2CONH2, M = 60 g mol−1) is dissolved in 125 g of water. The elevation in the boiling point of water is (Kb of water = 0.52 K kg mol−1)
  1. 0.744 K
  2. 0.208 K
  3. 0.104 K
  4. 0.416 K
Concept to recallΔTb = i Kb m, with molality m = (moles of solute)/(kg of solvent); urea is a non-electrolyte, i = 1.
  1. Moles of urea = 3.0/60 = 0.050 mol; mass of water = 0.125 kg.
  2. m = 0.050/0.125 = 0.40 mol kg−1.
  3. ΔTb = 0.52 × 0.40 = 0.208 K.

Answer: option (2) — 0.208 K

Trap0.104 K takes M(urea) as 120; 0.416 K puts i = 2 as though urea ionised; 0.744 K uses the freezing-point constant 1.86 instead of Kb.
Question 2Solutions
A solution of 1.26 g of a protein in 200 mL of water has an osmotic pressure of 2.57 × 10−3 bar at 300 K. The molar mass of the protein is approximately (R = 0.083 L bar K−1 mol−1)
  1. 6.1 × 107 g mol−1
  2. 6.1 × 104 g mol−1
  3. 5.5 × 103 g mol−1
  4. 61 g mol−1
Concept to recallπ = CRT = (w/M)RT/V, so M = wRT/(πV), with V in litres when R is in L bar K−1 mol−1. Osmotic pressure is the colligative property used for macromolecules because it is measured at room temperature and is large enough to read.
  1. M = wRT/(πV) = (1.26 × 0.083 × 300)/(2.57 × 10−3 × 0.200).
  2. Numerator = 31.37; denominator = 5.14 × 10−4.
  3. M = 6.1 × 104 g mol−1.

Answer: option (2) — 6.1 × 104 g mol−1

Trap5.5 × 103 puts T = 27 (°C) into the formula; 61 uses V = 200 (mL) instead of 0.200 L; 6.1 × 107 converts the volume the wrong way (0.2 × 10−3, as if m3).
Question 3Solutions
Two liquids A and B form an ideal solution. At 320 K the vapour pressures of pure A and pure B are 450 mm Hg and 700 mm Hg. A liquid mixture of A and B has a total vapour pressure of 600 mm Hg at this temperature. The mole fraction of A in the vapour in equilibrium with this mixture is
  1. 0.30
  2. 0.40
  3. 0.60
  4. 0.70
Concept to recallRaoult: ptotal = p°B + (p°A − p°B)xA. Dalton: yA = pA/ptotal = xAp°A/ptotal. The vapour is richer in the more volatile component.
  1. 600 = 700 + (450 − 700)xA ⇒ 250 xA = 100 ⇒ xA = 0.40.
  2. pA = 0.40 × 450 = 180 mm Hg.
  3. yA = 180/600 = 0.30.

Answer: option (1) — 0.30

Trap0.40 is xA, the composition of the liquid; 0.60 is xB; 0.70 is yB, the vapour fraction of the other component.
Question 4Solutions
Consider the following statements about non-ideal solutions:
  1. (A) A solution showing positive deviation from Raoult’s law forms a minimum-boiling azeotrope.
  2. (B) For a solution showing negative deviation, ΔmixH is positive.
  3. (C) KH of a gas increases with temperature, so the solubility of the gas in water decreases on heating.
  4. (D) A mixture of ethanol and acetone shows negative deviation from Raoult’s law.
  5. (E) A mixture of chloroform and acetone shows negative deviation from Raoult’s law.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (A), (B) and (D) only
  2. (B), (C) and (E) only
  3. (A), (D) and (E) only
  4. (A), (C) and (E) only
Concept to recallPositive deviation: A–B attractions weaker than A–A and B–B, ΔmixH > 0, higher vapour pressure, minimum-boiling azeotrope. Negative deviation: stronger A–B attraction (often a new H-bond), ΔmixH < 0, maximum-boiling azeotrope.
  1. (A) true: a higher vapour pressure means a lower boiling point, so the azeotrope boils lowest.
  2. (B) false: stronger A–B attraction releases heat, so ΔmixH < 0.
  3. (C) true: p = KHx; a larger KH at the same p means a smaller x.
  4. (D) false: acetone breaks the H-bonds of ethanol, a positive deviation.
  5. (E) true: CHCl3 forms an H-bond to the C=O of acetone, a negative deviation.

Answer: option (4) — (A), (C) and (E) only

TrapEthanol–acetone and chloroform–acetone are the NCERT pair set to be swapped; a bigger KH means a less soluble gas, not a more soluble one.
Question 5Solutions
Which of the following aqueous solutions has the lowest freezing point?
  1. 0.15 m NaCl
  2. 0.25 m glucose
  3. 0.10 m K3[Fe(CN)6]
  4. 0.10 m BaCl2
Concept to recallΔTf = i Kf m: the solution with the largest i × m (total moles of particles per kg) freezes lowest.
  1. K3[Fe(CN)6] → 3K+ + [Fe(CN)6]3−: i = 4, i m = 0.40.
  2. BaCl2: i = 3, i m = 0.30. NaCl: i = 2, i m = 0.30. Glucose: i = 1, i m = 0.25.
  3. Largest i m is the complex salt, so its freezing point is lowest.

Answer: option (3) — 0.10 m K3[Fe(CN)6]

TrapThe complex ion stays intact: counting K3[Fe(CN)6] as 10 particles (3 K + Fe + 6 CN) gets the right answer for the wrong reason; picking glucose for its larger molality ignores i.
Question 6Electrochemistry
For the cell Zn(s) | Zn2+(0.0050 M) || Ag+(0.050 M) | Ag(s) at 298 K, E°cell = 1.56 V. The emf of the cell is (2.303RT/F = 0.059 V, log 2 = 0.301)
  1. 1.57 V
  2. 1.55 V
  3. 1.59 V
  4. 1.54 V
Concept to recallNernst: E = E° − (0.059/n) log Q, with Q = [products]/[reactants] written from the balanced cell reaction, each concentration raised to its coefficient.
  1. Cell reaction: Zn + 2Ag+ → Zn2+ + 2Ag, n = 2.
  2. Q = [Zn2+]/[Ag+]2 = 0.0050/(0.050)2 = 2.
  3. E = 1.56 − (0.059/2)(0.301) = 1.56 − 0.0089 = 1.55 V.

Answer: option (2) — 1.55 V

Trap1.59 V forgets to square [Ag+] (Q = 0.1); 1.54 V uses n = 1; 1.57 V adds the correction instead of subtracting it (Q inverted).
Question 7Electrochemistry
The limiting molar conductivities (S cm2 mol−1) of sodium propanoate, nitric acid and sodium nitrate are 86.0, 421.0 and 121.5 respectively. The limiting molar conductivity of propanoic acid is
  1. 207.5 S cm2 mol−1
  2. 385.5 S cm2 mol−1
  3. 628.5 S cm2 mol−1
  4. 456.5 S cm2 mol−1
Concept to recallKohlrausch’s law of independent migration: Λ°m is a sum of ionic contributions, so a weak electrolyte’s value is built from strong ones: add the two that contain the wanted ions and subtract the one that supplies the unwanted pair.
  1. Λ°(C2H5COOH) = λ°(H+) + λ°(C2H5COO−).
  2. = Λ°(HNO3) + Λ°(C2H5COONa) − Λ°(NaNO3).
  3. = 421.0 + 86.0 − 121.5 = 385.5 S cm2 mol−1.

Answer: option (2) — 385.5 S cm2 mol−1

Trap628.5 adds all three; 456.5 subtracts the sodium propanoate instead of the sodium nitrate; 207.5 adds the two sodium salts, which contain no H+ at all.
Question 8Electrochemistry
A steady current of 2.0 A is passed through aqueous CuSO4. The time needed to deposit 1.27 g of copper at the cathode is (Cu = 63.5 g mol−1, F = 96500 C mol−1)
  1. 1930 s
  2. 965 s
  3. 3860 s
  4. 7720 s
Concept to recallFaraday’s first law: Q = It = neF, where ne = moles of metal × electrons per ion (Cu2+ + 2e− → Cu).
  1. Moles of Cu = 1.27/63.5 = 0.020 mol; electrons = 2 × 0.020 = 0.040 mol.
  2. Q = 0.040 × 96500 = 3860 C.
  3. t = Q/I = 3860/2.0 = 1930 s.

Answer: option (1) — 1930 s

Trap965 s takes Cu2+ as a one-electron reduction; 3860 is the charge in coulombs, not the time; 7720 s multiplies by the current instead of dividing.

Question 10Electrochemistry
Concentrated aqueous NaCl (brine) is electrolysed with inert electrodes. The products at the cathode and the anode respectively are
  1. Na and O2
  2. H2 and Cl2
  3. Na and Cl2
  4. H2 and O2
Concept to recallIn water the cathode reduces whichever species has the higher reduction potential (water rather than Na+, E° = −2.71 V); at the anode Cl− is oxidised in preference to water because of the overpotential for O2.
  1. Cathode: 2H2O + 2e− → H2 + 2OH− (Na+ is far harder to reduce).
  2. Anode: 2Cl− → Cl2 + 2e−; O2 evolution needs an overpotential.
  3. NaOH is left in solution.

Answer: option (2) — H2 and Cl2

TrapSodium metal is obtained only from molten NaCl; O2 at the anode is what E° alone predicts, but overpotential makes Cl2 win.
Question 11Chemical Kinetics
A first-order reaction has k = 2.31 × 10−3 s−1. The time taken for the reactant concentration to fall to one-eighth of its initial value is
  1. 600 s
  2. 900 s
  3. 300 s
  4. 2400 s
Concept to recallFor first order t1/2 = 0.693/k, independent of concentration; falling to (1/2)n of the start takes n half-lives.
  1. t1/2 = 0.693/(2.31 × 10−3) = 300 s.
  2. 1/8 = (1/2)3, so three half-lives are needed.
  3. t = 3 × 300 = 900 s.

Answer: option (2) — 900 s

Trap300 s is one half-life; 2400 s multiplies the half-life by 8 instead of 3; 600 s stops at one-quarter.
Question 12Chemical Kinetics
The plot of log k against 1/T for a reaction is a straight line with slope −4.0 × 103 K, as shown. The activation energy of the reaction is (R = 8.314 J K−1 mol−1)
  1. 176 kJ mol−1
  2. 76.6 kJ mol−1
  3. 33.3 kJ mol−1
  4. 9.21 kJ mol−1
Concept to recallArrhenius: log k = log A − Ea/(2.303 RT), so a plot of log k against 1/T has slope −Ea/(2.303 R). (A plot of ln k has slope −Ea/R.)
  1. Ea = −2.303 R × slope = 2.303 × 8.314 × 4.0 × 103 J mol−1.
  2. = 7.66 × 104 J mol−1 = 76.6 kJ mol−1.

Answer: option (2) — 76.6 kJ mol−1

Trap33.3 kJ treats the plot as ln k (drops the 2.303); 9.21 kJ leaves out R; 176 kJ multiplies by 2.303 twice.
Question 13Chemical Kinetics
Consider the following statements about chemical kinetics:
  1. (A) The order of a reaction can be zero or fractional.
  2. (B) The molecularity of an elementary reaction can be zero.
  3. (C) For a zero-order reaction, the half-life is directly proportional to the initial concentration.
  4. (D) The unit of the rate constant of a second-order reaction is mol−1 L s−1.
  5. (E) The rate constant of a first-order reaction depends on the initial concentration of the reactant.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (A), (C) and (D) only
  2. (A), (B) and (C) only
  3. (B), (D) and (E) only
  4. (A), (C) and (E) only
Concept to recallOrder is experimental (any value, including 0 and fractions); molecularity is the number of species colliding in an elementary step (1, 2 or rarely 3, never 0 or a fraction). Units of k: (mol L−1)1−n s−1.
  1. (A) true. (B) false: at least one molecule must take part.
  2. (C) true: t1/2 = [A]0/2k.
  3. (D) true: n = 2 gives (mol L−1)−1 s−1.

Question 14Chemical Kinetics
The rate law for a reaction is rate = k[A]2[B]. If the concentration of A is tripled and that of B is halved, the rate becomes
  1. 4.5 times
  2. 1.5 times
  3. 9 times
  4. 6 times
Concept to recallScale each concentration factor by its own order and multiply: new rate/old rate = (a)m(b)n.
  1. Factor from A: 32 = 9.
  2. Factor from B: (1/2)1 = 0.5.
  3. Rate changes by 9 × 0.5 = 4.5 times.

Answer: option (1) — 4.5 times

Trap1.5 treats A as first order; 9 ignores the change in B; 6 treats A as first order and doubles for B instead of halving (3 × 2).
Question 15Chemical Kinetics
For a reaction A → products, a plot of the half-life t1/2 against the initial concentration [A]0 is a straight line passing through the origin. The order of the reaction is
  1. first
  2. second
  3. third
  4. zero
Concept to recallt1/2 ∝ [A]01−n: zero order t1/2 = [A]0/2k; first order constant; second order ∝ 1/[A]0.
  1. A straight line through the origin means t1/2 ∝ [A]01.
  2. 1 − n = 1 ⇒ n = 0: zero order.

Answer: option (4) — zero

TrapFirst order gives a horizontal line (t1/2 independent of [A]0); second order gives a line through the origin only when t1/2 is plotted against 1/[A]0.

Question 20The d- and f-Block Elements
Which of the following is a consequence of the lanthanoid contraction?
  1. Lanthanoids show +3 as their common oxidation state
  2. Actinoids are radioactive
  3. Zn, Cd and Hg have low melting points
  4. Zr and Hf have almost identical atomic radii
Concept to recallThe poor shielding by 4f electrons makes radii fall steadily across the lanthanoids; the 5d series that follows is therefore about the same size as the 4d series above it.
  1. After the 14 lanthanoids, the extra contraction cancels the expected size increase from 4d to 5d.

Answer: option (4) — Zr and Hf have almost identical atomic radii

Question 21Coordination Compounds

  3. −2.4 Δo + 2P

Question 26Haloalkanes and Haloarenes
Passage: two routes for nucleophilic substitution

Haloalkanes react with nucleophiles by two limiting mechanisms. In the SN2 route the nucleophile attacks the carbon from the side opposite the leaving halogen; the C–Nu bond forms as the C–X bond breaks, in a single step through a five-coordinate transition state. The rate depends on both reactants, rate = k[RX][Nu−], and bulky groups round the reacting carbon slow it down sharply, so the order of reactivity is methyl > primary > secondary > tertiary. Because the nucleophile enters from the back, the three other groups are pushed over like an umbrella in a gale and a chiral centre is inverted.

In the SN1 route the C–X bond first ionises, slowly, to give a planar carbocation, which the nucleophile then captures quickly from either face. The rate depends on the haloalkane alone, rate = k[RX]; polar protic solvents such as water and alcohols help by solvating the ions. The more stable the carbocation, the faster the reaction, so tertiary halides react fastest, and benzylic and allylic halides, whose cations are stabilised by resonance, are also very reactive. Attack on both faces of the planar ion gives a largely racemic product from an optically active halide.

Question 49Biomolecules
Which of the following is a reducing sugar?
  1. Sucrose
  2. Starch
  3. Cellulose
  4. Maltose
Concept to recallA sugar reduces Tollens’ and Fehling’s reagents if it has a free hemiacetal (or hemiketal) carbon that can open to a free CHO or C=O.
  1. Maltose: C1 of one glucose is linked to C4 of the other; the second unit’s C1 is free → reducing.
  2. Sucrose: C1 (glucose) – C2 (fructose) link uses both reducing groups → non-reducing.
  3. Starch and cellulose are polysaccharides with no effective free reducing end → non-reducing.

Answer: option (4) — Maltose

TrapSucrose is the everyday sugar and is set as the tempting choice, but its glycosidic bond locks both anomeric carbons.
Question 50Biomolecules
A sample of double-stranded DNA contains 18% adenine. The percentage of cytosine in it is
  1. 64%
  2. 82%
  3. 32%
  4. 18%
Concept to recallBase pairing in the double helix: A pairs with T and G with C, so %A = %T and %G = %C (Chargaff’s rule), and all four add up to 100%.
  1. %T = %A = 18%, so A + T = 36%.
  2. G + C = 100 − 36 = 64%.
  3. %C = 64/2 = 32%.

Answer: option (3) — 32%

Trap18% equates C with A; 64% is G + C together; 82% is 100 − 18, forgetting the thymine.
