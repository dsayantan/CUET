Question 1Solutions
Which of the following ways of expressing the concentration of a solution does not change when the temperature changes?
  1. Molality
  2. Molarity
  3. Normality
  4. Mass by volume percentage (w/V)
Concept to recallA concentration term that is defined with masses or moles only (molality, mole fraction, mass %, ppm by mass) is temperature-independent; one that has the volume of solution in it changes, because liquids expand on heating.
  1. Molality = moles of solute / mass of solvent in kg: no volume anywhere.
  2. Molarity, normality and w/V all divide by the volume of solution, which grows with temperature.

Answer: option (1) — Molality

TrapMolarity is the commonest unit, so it is the commonest wrong pick; normality and w/V fail for the same reason, both being “per litre”.
Question 2Solutions
Assuming complete dissociation, the depression in freezing point of a 0.10 m aqueous solution of K2SO4 is (Kf for water = 1.86 K kg mol−1)
  1. 0.186 K
  2. 0.372 K
  3. 0.558 K
  4. 0.062 K
Concept to recallΔTf = i Kf m; for complete dissociation i = the number of ions per formula unit.
  1. K2SO4 → 2K+ + SO42−, so i = 3.
  2. ΔTf = 3 × 1.86 × 0.10 = 0.558 K.

Answer: option (3) — 0.558 K

Trap0.186 K forgets i (treats the salt as a non-electrolyte); 0.372 K counts “two kinds of ion” instead of three ions; 0.062 K divides by i instead of multiplying.

Question 4Solutions
Consider the following statements about the solubility of gases in liquids.
  1. (A) The solubility of a gas in a liquid increases as the partial pressure of the gas above it increases.
  2. (B) At a given pressure, the larger the Henry’s law constant KH of a gas, the more soluble it is.
  3. (C) KH increases with temperature, so gases are less soluble in water at a higher temperature.
  4. (D) The “bends” suffered by scuba divers are caused by nitrogen coming out of the blood as bubbles when the diver surfaces.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (A) and (B) only
  2. (A), (B) and (D) only
  3. (B), (C) and (D) only
  4. (A), (C) and (D) only
Concept to recallHenry’s law in NCERT form: p = KH x. For a fixed partial pressure p, the mole fraction dissolved x = p/KH: a large KH means low solubility.
  1. (A) true: x ∝ p.
  2. (B) false: x = p/KH, so a larger KH gives a smaller x.
  3. (C) true: NCERT’s table shows KH rising with T, i.e. gases dissolve less in warm water (why aquatic life prefers cold water).
  4. (D) true: deep down, high pressure dissolves extra N2; on rising, it comes out as bubbles.

Answer: option (4) — (A), (C) and (D) only

TrapIn the p = KHx form, KH sits on the pressure side, so it measures how unwilling the gas is to dissolve. Reading it as a solubility constant makes (B) look true.
Question 5Solutions
18 g of glucose (M = 180 g mol−1) is dissolved in 178.2 g of water. The vapour pressure of the solution at 373 K, where pure water boils at 760 mm Hg, is
  1. 752.4 mm Hg
  2. 7.6 mm Hg
  3. 683.2 mm Hg
  4. 767.6 mm Hg
Concept to recallRaoult’s law for a non-volatile solute: p = xsolvent p°, equivalently (p° − p)/p° = xsolute (relative lowering of vapour pressure).
  1. Moles of glucose = 18/180 = 0.1; moles of water = 178.2/18 = 9.9; total 10.0.
  2. xwater = 9.9/10.0 = 0.99.
  3. p = 0.99 × 760 = 752.4 mm Hg (a lowering of 7.6 mm Hg).

Answer: option (1) — 752.4 mm Hg

Trap7.6 mm Hg is the lowering, not the vapour pressure; 683.2 mm Hg uses the mass ratio 18/178.2 as if it were a mole fraction; 767.6 mm Hg adds the lowering instead of subtracting it.
Question 6Electrochemistry
The SI unit of molar conductivity (Λm) is
  1. S m−1
  2. S m−2 mol
  3. S m2 mol−1
  4. Ω m2 mol−1
Concept to recallΛm = κ/c: conductivity (S m−1) divided by concentration (mol m−3).
  1. (S m−1) / (mol m−3) = S m2 mol−1.
  2. In the cm-based units NCERT also uses: S cm2 mol−1.

Answer: option (3) — S m2 mol−1

TrapS m−1 is the unit of conductivity κ, not of molar conductivity; Ω is resistance, the reciprocal of siemens.
Question 7Electrochemistry
The limiting molar conductivities (in S cm2 mol−1) of HCl, NaCl and CH3COONa are 426.0, 126.0 and 91.0 respectively. The limiting molar conductivity of CH3COOH, in the same unit, is
  1. 461.0
  2. 391.0
  3. 643.0
  4. 209.0
Concept to recallKohlrausch’s law: Λ°m of an electrolyte is the sum of the limiting ionic conductivities of its ions. Combine the strong electrolytes so that the unwanted ions cancel.
  1. Λ°(CH3COOH) = λ°(H+) + λ°(CH3COO−)
  2. = Λ°(HCl) + Λ°(CH3COONa) − Λ°(NaCl)
  3. = 426.0 + 91.0 − 126.0 = 391.0 S cm2 mol−1.

Answer: option (2) — 391.0

Trap461.0 subtracts the wrong salt (HCl + NaCl − CH3COONa leaves Na+ and Cl− twice); 643.0 adds all three; 209.0 subtracts both from HCl.
Question 8Electrochemistry
The quantity of electricity needed to reduce 1 mol of Cr2O72− to Cr3+ is (1 F = 96500 C mol−1)
  1. 2.90 × 105 C
  2. 1.93 × 105 C
  3. 9.65 × 104 C
  4. 5.79 × 105 C
Concept to recallFaraday’s first law: charge = (moles of electrons) × F. Count electrons from the change in oxidation state times the number of atoms that change.
  1. Cr goes from +6 to +3: 3 electrons per Cr atom.
  2. There are 2 Cr atoms in the ion: 6 mol e− per mol of Cr2O72−.
  3. Charge = 6 × 96500 = 5.79 × 105 C.

Answer: option (4) — 5.79 × 105 C

Trap2.90 × 105 C counts one Cr only (3 F); 1.93 × 105 C uses the ionic charge 2 as the electron count; 9.65 × 104 C is a single faraday.
Question 9Electrochemistry
For the cell Zn | Zn2+(0.1 M) || Cu2+(0.001 M) | Cu, E°cell = 1.10 V. Taking 2.303RT/F = 0.059 V at 298 K, the emf of the cell is
  1. 1.041 V
  2. 1.159 V
  3. 0.982 V
  4. 1.071 V
Concept to recallNernst: Ecell = E°cell − (0.059/n) log ([product ion]/[reactant ion]); for Zn + Cu2+ → Zn2+ + Cu, Q = [Zn2+]/[Cu2+] and n = 2.
  1. Q = 0.1/0.001 = 100, log Q = 2.
  2. E = 1.10 − (0.059/2)(2) = 1.10 − 0.059 = 1.041 V.
  3. Sense check: the oxidised side (Zn2+) is richer than the reduced side, so E falls below E°.

Answer: option (1) — 1.041 V

Trap1.159 V inverts Q ([Cu2+]/[Zn2+]); 0.982 V takes n = 1; 1.071 V uses log Q = 1, reading 0.1/0.001 as 10.
Question 10Electrochemistry
Consider the following statements.
  1. (A) The standard Gibbs energy of a cell reaction is related to the standard emf by ΔrG° = −nFE°cell.
  2. (B) A cell reaction is spontaneous when Ecell is positive.
  3. (C) Since E°cell of the Daniell cell is +1.10 V, the equilibrium constant of Zn + Cu2+ ⇒ Zn2+ + Cu is much greater than 1.
  4. (D) When aqueous CuSO4 is electrolysed between copper electrodes, oxygen is evolved at the anode.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (A), (B), (C) and (D)
  2. (A) and (D) only
  3. (B), (C) and (D) only
  4. (A), (B) and (C) only
Concept to recallΔrG° = −nFE° = −2.303RT log K, so a positive E° means a negative ΔrG° and K > 1. At an anode the most easily oxidised species reacts; an active (Cu) anode can itself be that species.
  1. (A) true.
  2. (B) true: E > 0 gives ΔG < 0.
  3. (C) true: log K = 2 × 1.10/0.059 ≈ 37, so K ≈ 1037.
  4. (D) false: the copper anode dissolves, Cu → Cu2+ + 2e−; O2 is evolved only with an inert (Pt) anode.

Answer: option (4) — (A), (B) and (C) only

TrapO2 at the anode is the inert-electrode answer; with copper electrodes the anode is consumed and copper is simply transferred to the cathode (the basis of electrorefining).
Question 11Chemical Kinetics
For the reaction R → P, the instantaneous rate at time t is given by
  1. +d[R]/dt, the slope of the tangent to the [R]–t curve at t
  2. −d[R]/dt, the negative of the slope of the tangent to the [R]–t curve at t
  3. Δ[R]/Δt taken over the whole course of the reaction
  4. [R]/t at that instant
Concept to recallAverage rate = −Δ[R]/Δt over an interval; instantaneous rate is its limit as Δt → 0, i.e. −d[R]/dt = +d[P]/dt, read off as the slope of the tangent.
  1. [R] falls with time, so d[R]/dt is negative; the minus sign makes the rate positive.

Answer: option (2) — −d[R]/dt, the negative of the slope of the tangent to the [R]–t curve at t

TrapWithout the minus sign the rate comes out negative; an interval over the whole reaction gives the average rate.
Question 12Chemical Kinetics
The rate constant of a first-order reaction is 5.0 × 10−3 s−1. Its half-life is
  1. 200 s
  2. 69.3 s
  3. 138.6 s
  4. 13.86 s
Concept to recallFirst order: t½ = 0.693/k, independent of the initial concentration.
  1. t½ = 0.693/(5.0 × 10−3) = 138.6 s.

Answer: option (3) — 138.6 s

Trap200 s is 1/k (the 0.693 dropped); 69.3 s halves it again; 13.86 s is a power-of-ten slip.
Question 13Chemical Kinetics
For a first-order reaction, the ratio of the time required for 99.9% completion to the time required for 50% completion is close to
  1. 10
  2. 3
  3. 2
  4. 999
Concept to recallFirst order: t = (2.303/k) log([R]0/[R]). Every fraction is set by the ratio [R]0/[R] alone.
  1. 99.9% complete: [R]0/[R] = 1000, so t = (2.303/k) × 3.
  2. 50% complete: t½ = (2.303/k) × log 2 = (2.303/k) × 0.301.
  3. Ratio = 3/0.301 ≈ 10: practically complete after about ten half-lives.

Answer: option (1) — 10

Trap3 compares log 1000 with log 10 instead of log 2; 2 treats 99.9% as “twice 50%”; 999 compares the fractions reacted, not the logarithms.

Question 15Chemical Kinetics
For a first-order reaction, log([R]0/[R]) is plotted against time t as shown. The slope of the line is
  1. −k/2.303
  2. k/2.303
  3. k
  4. 2.303/k
Concept to recallIntegrated first-order law: k = (2.303/t) log([R]0/[R]), i.e. log([R]0/[R]) = (k/2.303)t — a straight line through the origin.

Question 17The d- and f-Block Elements
Manganese shows its highest oxidation state, +7, in the oxide Mn2O7, but its highest fluoride is only MnF4. The reason is that
  1. oxygen can form multiple bonds to the metal
  2. fluorine is less electronegative than oxygen
  3. fluorine atoms are larger than oxygen atoms
  4. oxygen has a lower electron affinity than fluorine
Concept to recallNCERT: the ability of oxygen to stabilise high oxidation states exceeds that of fluorine because oxygen forms multiple bonds to metals (Mn=O in Mn2O7).
  1. In Mn2O7 each Mn is tetrahedrally surrounded by O, with Mn=O double bonds and one Mn–O–Mn bridge.
  2. Seven fluorines, each forming only a single bond, cannot crowd round one Mn atom; +4 is the limit.

Answer: option (1) — oxygen can form multiple bonds to the metal

TrapFluorine is more electronegative and smaller than oxygen; those facts would favour F, not O. Electron affinity is not the deciding factor.

Question 23Coordination Compounds
The number of geometrical isomers of the square-planar complex [Pt(NH3)(Br)(Cl)(py)] is
  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 0
Concept to recallSquare-planar [MABXL] (four different monodentate ligands) has three geometrical isomers: fix one ligand and place each of the other three opposite to it in turn.
  1. Fix NH3. The ligand trans to it can be Br, Cl or py.
  2. Once the trans partner is chosen, the remaining two are cis to NH3, and swapping them only gives the same molecule turned over.
  3. Hence 3 isomers. A square-planar complex is flat, so it has no optical isomers.

Answer: option (2) — 3

Trap2 carries over the cis/trans pair of [MA2B2]; 0 thinks of a tetrahedral complex, which shows no geometrical isomerism.

  2. (ii) It reacts with hydroxylamine to form an oxime and adds HCN to form a cyanohydrin.
  3. (iii) Bromine water oxidises it to gluconic acid, C6H12O7.
  4. (iv) Nitric acid oxidises it to saccharic acid, a dicarboxylic acid.
  5. (v) Acetic anhydride converts it to a pentaacetate.

Some later observations could not be explained by this structure, and glucose was found to exist mainly as two cyclic forms, called α and β.

In the cyclic forms of glucose mentioned at the end of Passage II, the −CHO group at C-1 has combined with the −OH group at ____, and the α and β forms are called ____.
  1. C-4; anomers
  2. C-5; anomers
  3. C-5; enantiomers
  4. C-6; epimers
Concept to recallGlucose forms a six-membered hemiacetal ring (pyranose) between C-1 and the C-5 −OH. The new chiral centre at C-1 gives two isomers, α and β, which differ only there: anomers.
  1. C-1 to C-5 through O gives a ring of five carbons + one oxygen: six-membered, like pyran.
  2. α- and β-D-glucose are not mirror images of each other; they differ at C-1 only.

Answer: option (2) — C-5; anomers

TrapC-4 would give a five-membered (furanose) ring, which is fructose’s ring; enantiomers are mirror images, which α and β are not; C-6 carries the −CH2OH that stays outside the ring.
Question 50Biomolecules
Consider the following statements.
  1. (A) DNA contains the sugar 2-deoxyribose, while RNA contains ribose.
  2. (B) Uracil is found in RNA in place of the thymine of DNA.
  3. (C) In DNA, adenine pairs with thymine through two hydrogen bonds and guanine with cytosine through three.
  4. (D) The α-helix is the tertiary structure of a protein.
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
  1. (A), (B), (C) and (D)
  2. (B) and (D) only
  3. (A) and (C) only
  4. (A), (B) and (C) only
Concept to recallProtein structure: primary (sequence), secondary (α-helix, β-pleated sheet, held by H-bonds), tertiary (overall folding), quaternary (several subunits). DNA: deoxyribose with A, G, C, T; RNA: ribose with A, G, C, U.
  1. (A) true.
  2. (B) true: RNA bases are A, G, C and U; DNA has T instead of U.
  3. (C) true: this complementary pairing holds the two strands of the double helix together.
  4. (D) false: the α-helix is a secondary structure.

Answer: option (4) — (A), (B) and (C) only

Trapα-Helix and β-sheet are both secondary; “tertiary” is the further folding of those helices and sheets.
